\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

\sysname{} solves the sparse-observation TE problem of
\cref{sec:sparse-te} in four stages, as illustrated in
\cref{fig:arch}. Given the sparse observation $(O^t, M)$ and history $H^t$,
(i) unmeasured demands are represented by a learnable mask embedding
(\cref{sec:mask-embed}); (ii) a mask-aware gated GNN propagates information
between candidate paths and links without letting placeholders contaminate
link states (\cref{sec:gating}); (iii) a Transformer encodes the observation
history and fuses it with spatial embeddings via per-demand cross-attention
(\cref{sec:fusion}); (iv) a shared policy head outputs split ratios per
demand, refined by ADMM (\cref{sec:training}). All learnable weights are
shared across demands and independent of the demand-set size, which
\cref{sec:scaling} exploits for zero-retraining deployment under
constellation dynamics. \Cref{alg:infer} summarizes one inference epoch.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\sysname{} inference at control epoch $t$}
\label{alg:infer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require sparse history $H^t$, mask $M$, topology $G^t$, weights $\theta^\star$
\Ensure split ratios $\{r_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{K}^t}$
\State $\mathcal{K}^t \gets$ active demand set from recent history
    \Comment{pruning, \cref{sec:scaling}}
\State rebuild bipartite graph and gated adjacency $\tilde{A}$ from
    $\mathcal{K}^t, M$ \Comment{\cref{eq:gate}}
\State $x_i \gets M_i d_i + (1-M_i)\phi$ for all $i$
    \Comment{mask embedding, \cref{eq:mask-embed}}
\State $H_S \gets \textsc{GatedFlowGNN}(x, \tilde{A})$
    \Comment{spatial embeddings}
\State $H_T \gets \textsc{TemporalEncoder}(H^t)$
    \Comment{temporal embeddings}
\State $H_F \gets \textsc{CrossAttention}(H_T, H_S)$ \Comment{\cref{eq:fusion}}
\State $\{r_i\} \gets \textsc{PolicyHead}([H_S \,\|\, H_F])$
\State $\{r_i\} \gets \textsc{ADMMRepair}(\{r_i\}, G^t)$
    \Comment{\cref{sec:training}}
\State \Return $\{r_i\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Mask-Aware Demand Representation}
\label{sec:mask-embed}

A sparse observation is fundamentally ambiguous: a zero entry may mean ``no
traffic'' or ``not measured''. Zero-filling --- the de-facto treatment in
prior sparse TE~\cite{test} --- collapses the two cases and systematically
biases the allocator toward starving unmeasured demands. \sysname{} instead
assigns each unmeasured demand a \emph{learnable mask embedding}
$\phi \in \mathbb{R}^{\kappa}$ (one scalar per candidate path):
\begin{equation}
    x_i = M_i \cdot d_i + (1 - M_i) \cdot \phi,
    \label{eq:mask-embed}
\end{equation}
so the model learns a data-driven prior for missing traffic during
end-to-end training, and --- critically --- the input itself distinguishes
``not measured'' from ``zero''. Learnable imputation has proven effective
for incomplete graph features~\cite{t2gnn}; we bring it to TE, where the
imputed value directly steers resource allocation. The embedding is trained
jointly with the policy under the TE objective~\eqref{eq:rl-obj} rather than
a reconstruction loss, so $\phi$ converges to values that maximize
allocation quality, not reconstruction accuracy.

\subsection{Gated Message Passing}
\label{sec:gating}

\sysname{} inherits Teal's flow-centric graph~\cite{teal}: candidate paths
and physical links form a bipartite structure where a PathNode connects to
the EdgeNodes it traverses, and message passing alternates between them.
Under sparse observation, however, symmetric message passing is harmful:
placeholder values on unmeasured PathNodes would propagate into EdgeNode
embeddings and corrupt the network-wide estimate of link utilization --- a
phenomenon consistent with the feature--structure interference observed in
incomplete graphs~\cite{t2gnn}. We therefore gate the propagation
\emph{asymmetrically}. Let $A$ be the normalized bipartite adjacency; we
zero out entries that carry path-to-edge messages from unmeasured paths
while keeping all edge-to-path entries:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{A}[e, p] = M_p \cdot A[e, p], \qquad
    \tilde{A}[p, e] = A[p, e],
    \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $M_p$ is the demand-level mask lifted to path $p$. Unmeasured demands
thus remain \emph{listeners}: they sense link states --- which are shaped by
measured traffic --- but inject no fabricated volume into them. The gate is
a fixed binary function of the mask with no extra parameters, adding zero
inference overhead. The asymmetric gate yields a formal isolation property:

\begin{proposition}[Placeholder isolation]
\label{prop:isolation}
Under the gated propagation~\eqref{eq:gate}, the embeddings of all
EdgeNodes and of all PathNodes belonging to measured demands are
independent of the mask embedding $\phi$; $\phi$ influences only the
allocations of unmeasured demands.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By induction on GNN layers. At layer $0$, only unmeasured PathNodes carry
$\phi$. At layer $l$, an EdgeNode aggregates path-to-edge messages with
weights $\tilde{A}[e,p] = M_p A[e,p]$, which vanish for every unmeasured
path; hence EdgeNode embeddings depend only on measured-path inputs and
their own history, none of which contains $\phi$. A measured PathNode
aggregates EdgeNode embeddings ($\phi$-free by the above) and its own
$\phi$-free input; the per-demand layers mix only paths of the same demand,
which share the same mask value. Thus $\phi$ never enters any
$\phi$-free branch, and only unmeasured PathNodes --- and consequently
their own split ratios --- depend on $\phi$.
\end{IEEEproof}

In other words, learning a poor prior $\phi$ can at worst misallocate the
unmeasured demands themselves, but can never corrupt the allocations of
measured traffic --- a robustness guarantee that zero-filling with
ungated propagation does not provide.

\subsection{Temporal-Spatial Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

Satellite traffic exhibits strong short-term temporal correlation; the
recent history of sparse observations therefore carries recoverable
information about currently missing entries. \sysname{} encodes, for each
candidate path, its observation sequence over the last $L$ epochs with a
lightweight Transformer: scalar volumes are log-compressed --- which we find
essential for training stability --- and projected to $d_{\mathrm{model}}$
dimensions, summed with learnable positional embeddings, and passed through
a Transformer encoder; the representation at the latest step forms the
temporal embedding $H_T$. Rather than concatenating temporal and spatial
features naively, we fuse them with \emph{per-demand cross-attention}:
within each demand, temporal embeddings of its $\kappa$ candidate paths act
as queries attending to the $\kappa$ spatial (GNN) embeddings $H_S$ as keys
and values,
\begin{equation}
    H_F = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(
        \frac{W_q H_T \, (W_k H_S)^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
    \right) W_v H_S,
    \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
and the fused features $[H_S \,\|\, H_F]$ feed the policy head. Intuitively,
the temporal stream decides \emph{which} spatial evidence to trust for each
path: when the current entry is missing, attention shifts toward paths and
links whose states corroborate the historical pattern. The whole module adds
\todo{XX}~ms to inference on our largest constellation
(\cref{sec:eval}).

\subsection{Scaling to Dynamic Constellations}
\label{sec:scaling}

\subsubsection{Demand Pruning}
Modeling all $N(N-1)$ pairs of a 1,584-satellite constellation yields 2.5M
demands and tens of millions of path variables --- infeasible for online
control. Satellite traffic, however, is geographically concentrated: only
0.25\% of pairs ever carry traffic in our dataset. \sysname{} instantiates
PathNodes only for the demand-pair union observed in historical traffic
matrices, shrinking the graph by ${\sim}400\times$ with no loss of served
traffic, in the same spirit as SaTE's traffic pruning~\cite{sate}.

\subsubsection{Zero-Retraining under Drift}
Pruned demand sets are not static: as satellites move, the active pair set
drifts (we measure ${\sim}10\%$ turnover across our trace,
\cref{sec:eval}). Rather than retraining, \sysname{} exploits a structural
property of its architecture: every learnable component --- GNN layers, mask
embedding, temporal encoder, fusion and policy heads --- is shared across
demands and independent of the demand-set size. The demand set, candidate
paths, and the bipartite graph are runtime \emph{inputs}, not parameters. At
each control epoch the controller rebuilds the graph from the current active
set --- a millisecond-scale index operation on +Grid constellations, where
candidate paths depend only on the orbital displacement between endpoints
and reduce to $O(N)$ path templates --- and reuses the trained weights
as-is. Demands outside the modeled set --- necessarily sporadic mice flows
--- fall back to shortest-path routing until the next set refresh.
This reuse is justified by the following structural property:

\begin{proposition}[Size invariance and permutation equivariance]
\label{prop:invariance}
The parameter set of \sysname{} is independent of $|\mathcal{K}^t|$, $N$,
and $|E^t|$, and the mapping from inputs to split ratios is equivariant to
any permutation of the demand indices.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Every learnable component is a per-node or per-demand operator with shared
weights: the mask embedding $\phi$ and temporal encoder act on each path
token, the GNN layers combine node-wise linear maps with sparse
aggregation, the fusion and policy heads act within each demand. None of
their parameter shapes involves $|\mathcal{K}^t|$, $N$, or $|E^t|$.
Permuting demand indices permutes the rows of every input and of the
bipartite adjacency consistently; since shared node-wise operators commute
with permutations and sparse aggregation is index-based, all intermediate
embeddings --- and hence the outputs --- are permuted identically.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Training and Constraint Repair}
\label{sec:training}

\sysname{} trains with the multi-agent RL scheme of Teal~\cite{teal}: each
demand is an agent sharing the policy network. The policy outputs a
Gaussian mean per path, and actions are sampled during training. To assign
credit, each agent receives a COMA-style counterfactual reward that
estimates its marginal contribution to the global
objective~\eqref{eq:rl-obj}: for demand $i$,
\begin{equation}
    A_i = \mathbb{E}_{\tilde{r}_i \sim \pi'}
        \bigl[ R(r_{-i}, r_i) - R(r_{-i}, \tilde{r}_i) \bigr],
    \label{eq:coma}
\end{equation}
where $r_{-i}$ denotes the allocations of all other demands and
$\tilde{r}_i$ is a counterfactual action resampled from a reference
distribution $\pi'$; the policy gradient weights each agent's log
probability by $A_i$. \Cref{alg:train} summarizes offline training. Two
points deserve emphasis. First, the reward is computed against
\emph{ground-truth} demands during offline training --- sparse observation
constrains the policy input, never the training signal --- which is exactly
what a simulator or historical trace provides. Second, we deliberately
retain reward-driven RL rather than imitation learning~\cite{fnc}:
imitation learning requires expert allocations solved from \emph{fully
realized} demands of each episode, a supervision signal that is unavailable
when even post-hoc observation is sparse; RL needs only the reward, which
the network itself provides. At inference, allocations are refined by two
parallelizable ADMM iterations to repair residual violations of the
capacity constraint~\eqref{eq:capacity}, adding less than 1~ms.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Offline training of \sysname{}}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require historical trace $\{(D^t, G^t)\}$, mask $M$, learning rate $\eta$
\Ensure trained weights $\theta^\star$ (incl.\ mask embedding $\phi$)
\State build demand set $\mathcal{K}$ and graph from training-slice TMs
\For{each epoch}
    \For{each snapshot $t$ in the training slice}
        \State form sparse input $(H^t, M)$;
            sample actions $r \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid H^t, M, G^t)$
        \State compute counterfactual rewards $\{A_i\}$
            via~\eqref{eq:coma} \textbf{using the true} $D^t$
        \State $\theta \gets \theta + \eta \sum_i A_i
            \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(r_i)$
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State \Return $\theta^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity Analysis}
\label{sec:complexity}

Let $D = |\mathcal{K}^t|$ be the number of active demands, $\kappa$ the
candidate paths per demand, $E$ the number of ISLs, and $\bar{\ell}$ the
average path length. The gated FlowGNN performs message passing over the
bipartite graph whose edge count is $O(D\kappa\bar{\ell})$; with $\Lambda$
layers its cost is $O(\Lambda D \kappa \bar{\ell} d)$ for hidden width $d$.
The temporal encoder applies a Transformer of length $L$ independently to
each of the $D\kappa$ path tokens, costing $O(D\kappa L^2 d)$, and the
per-demand cross-attention over $\kappa$ paths adds $O(D\kappa^2 d)$. Since
$\kappa$, $L$, $\bar{\ell}$, and $d$ are small constants fixed by design, the
end-to-end inference cost is \emph{linear} in the number of active demands,
$O(D)$, and all operations are batched across demands on the GPU.
Crucially, the parameter count is independent of $D$, $N$, and $E$: the same
weights apply to any demand set, which is what enables zero-retraining reuse
(\cref{sec:scaling}). Demand pruning reduces $D$ from $N(N-1)$ to the
observed support --- roughly $400\times$ on our constellation --- turning an
otherwise intractable problem into one solved in milliseconds
(\cref{sec:runtime}).
