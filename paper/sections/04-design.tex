\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

\sysname{} solves the sparse-observation TE problem of
\cref{sec:sparse-te} in four stages, as illustrated in
\cref{fig:arch}. Given the sparse observation $(O^t, M)$ and history $H^t$,
(i) unmeasured demands are represented by a learnable mask embedding
(\cref{sec:mask-embed}); (ii) a mask-aware gated GNN propagates information
between candidate paths and links without letting placeholders contaminate
link states (\cref{sec:gating}); (iii) a Transformer encodes the observation
history and fuses it with spatial embeddings via per-demand cross-attention
(\cref{sec:fusion}); (iv) a shared policy head outputs split ratios per
demand, refined by ADMM (\cref{sec:training}). All learnable weights are
shared across demands and independent of the demand-set size, which
\cref{sec:scaling} exploits for zero-retraining deployment under
constellation dynamics.

\subsection{Mask-Aware Demand Representation}
\label{sec:mask-embed}

A sparse observation is fundamentally ambiguous: a zero entry may mean ``no
traffic'' or ``not measured''. Zero-filling --- the de-facto treatment in
prior sparse TE~\cite{test} --- collapses the two cases and systematically
biases the allocator toward starving unmeasured demands. \sysname{} instead
assigns each unmeasured demand a \emph{learnable mask embedding}
$\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{\kappa}$ (one scalar per candidate path):
\begin{equation}
    x_i = M_i \cdot d_i + (1 - M_i) \cdot \theta,
    \label{eq:mask-embed}
\end{equation}
so the model learns a data-driven prior for missing traffic during
end-to-end training, and --- critically --- the input itself distinguishes
``not measured'' from ``zero''. Learnable imputation has proven effective
for incomplete graph features~\cite{t2gnn}; we bring it to TE, where the
imputed value directly steers resource allocation. The embedding is trained
jointly with the policy under the TE objective~\eqref{eq:rl-obj} rather than
a reconstruction loss, so $\theta$ converges to values that maximize
allocation quality, not reconstruction accuracy.

\subsection{Gated Message Passing}
\label{sec:gating}

\sysname{} inherits Teal's flow-centric graph~\cite{teal}: candidate paths
and physical links form a bipartite structure where a PathNode connects to
the EdgeNodes it traverses, and message passing alternates between them.
Under sparse observation, however, symmetric message passing is harmful:
placeholder values on unmeasured PathNodes would propagate into EdgeNode
embeddings and corrupt the network-wide estimate of link utilization --- a
phenomenon consistent with the feature--structure interference observed in
incomplete graphs~\cite{t2gnn}. We therefore gate the propagation
\emph{asymmetrically}. Let $A$ be the normalized bipartite adjacency; we
zero out entries that carry path-to-edge messages from unmeasured paths
while keeping all edge-to-path entries:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{A}[e, p] = M_p \cdot A[e, p], \qquad
    \tilde{A}[p, e] = A[p, e],
    \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $M_p$ is the demand-level mask lifted to path $p$. Unmeasured demands
thus remain \emph{listeners}: they sense link states --- which are shaped by
measured traffic --- but inject no fabricated volume into them. The gate is
a fixed binary function of the mask with no extra parameters, adding zero
inference overhead.

\subsection{Temporal-Spatial Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

Satellite traffic exhibits strong short-term temporal correlation; the
recent history of sparse observations therefore carries recoverable
information about currently missing entries. \sysname{} encodes, for each
candidate path, its observation sequence over the last $L$ epochs with a
lightweight Transformer: scalar volumes are log-compressed --- which we find
essential for training stability --- and projected to $d_{\mathrm{model}}$
dimensions, summed with learnable positional embeddings, and passed through
a Transformer encoder; the representation at the latest step forms the
temporal embedding $H_T$. Rather than concatenating temporal and spatial
features naively, we fuse them with \emph{per-demand cross-attention}:
within each demand, temporal embeddings of its $\kappa$ candidate paths act
as queries attending to the $\kappa$ spatial (GNN) embeddings $H_S$ as keys
and values,
\begin{equation}
    H_F = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(
        \frac{W_q H_T \, (W_k H_S)^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
    \right) W_v H_S,
    \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
and the fused features $[H_S \,\|\, H_F]$ feed the policy head. Intuitively,
the temporal stream decides \emph{which} spatial evidence to trust for each
path: when the current entry is missing, attention shifts toward paths and
links whose states corroborate the historical pattern. The whole module adds
\todo{XX}~ms to inference on our largest constellation
(\cref{sec:eval}).

\subsection{Scaling to Dynamic Constellations}
\label{sec:scaling}

\subsubsection{Demand Pruning}
Modeling all $N(N-1)$ pairs of a 1,584-satellite constellation yields 2.5M
demands and tens of millions of path variables --- infeasible for online
control. Satellite traffic, however, is geographically concentrated: only
0.25\% of pairs ever carry traffic in our dataset. \sysname{} instantiates
PathNodes only for the demand-pair union observed in historical traffic
matrices, shrinking the graph by ${\sim}400\times$ with no loss of served
traffic, in the same spirit as SaTE's traffic pruning~\cite{sate}.

\subsubsection{Zero-Retraining under Drift}
Pruned demand sets are not static: as satellites move, the active pair set
drifts (we measure ${\sim}10\%$ turnover across our trace,
\cref{sec:eval}). Rather than retraining, \sysname{} exploits a structural
property of its architecture: every learnable component --- GNN layers, mask
embedding, temporal encoder, fusion and policy heads --- is shared across
demands and independent of the demand-set size. The demand set, candidate
paths, and the bipartite graph are runtime \emph{inputs}, not parameters. At
each control epoch the controller rebuilds the graph from the current active
set --- a millisecond-scale index operation on +Grid constellations, where
candidate paths depend only on the orbital displacement between endpoints
and reduce to $O(N)$ path templates --- and reuses the trained weights
as-is. Demands outside the modeled set --- necessarily sporadic mice flows
--- fall back to shortest-path routing until the next set refresh.

\subsection{Training and Constraint Repair}
\label{sec:training}

\sysname{} trains with the multi-agent RL scheme of Teal~\cite{teal}: each
demand is an agent sharing the policy network, optimized by a COMA-style
reward that estimates each agent's marginal contribution to the global
objective~\eqref{eq:rl-obj}. Two points deserve emphasis. First, the reward
is computed against \emph{ground-truth} demands during offline training ---
sparse observation constrains the policy input, never the training signal
--- which is exactly what a simulator or historical trace provides. Second,
we deliberately retain reward-driven RL rather than imitation
learning~\cite{fnc}: imitation learning requires expert allocations solved
from \emph{fully realized} demands of each episode, a supervision signal
that is unavailable when even post-hoc observation is sparse; RL needs only
the reward, which the network itself provides. At inference, allocations are
refined by two parallelizable ADMM iterations to repair residual violations
of the capacity constraint~\eqref{eq:capacity}, adding less than 1~ms.
