\IEEEPARstart{L}{ow-Earth-orbit} (LEO) satellite constellations are rapidly
scaling toward tens of thousands of satellites~\cite{handley2018,
bhattacherjee2019}. Their inter-satellite links (ISLs) carry
spatially concentrated and temporally varying traffic under limited
capacity~\cite{kassing2020}. Traffic engineering (TE), which
coordinates how each demand is split across candidate paths, is therefore
a key control primitive~\cite{jain2013,hong2018}. Classical
optimization can take minutes at constellation scale, whereas learned
allocators reduce online decision time to the sub-second range by moving
most computation offline~\cite{teal,dote,sate}.

Existing learned TE systems nevertheless rely on a complete traffic matrix
at every control epoch. This assumption is particularly fragile in an LEO
constellation. Measuring every demand requires satellites to meter,
aggregate, and transmit fine-grained statistics through telemetry channels
that compete with user traffic for ISL and ground-station
bandwidth~\cite{kassing2020,michel2022}. It also consumes onboard
computation, storage, and power, while intermittent ground contacts
introduce unavoidable gaps; the expense of network-wide metering is the very
reason terrestrial operators have long inferred traffic matrices instead of
measuring them~\cite{vardi1996,zhang2003}. As illustrated in
\cref{fig:scenario}, only a subset of satellites may meter traffic at a
given epoch. The controller then receives a sparse traffic matrix but must
still allocate every demand. Efficient TE from sparse observations is thus
a natural operating mode for LEO networks, not merely a degraded version
of complete measurement.

Sparse observation creates three coupled challenges. First,
\emph{unmeasured} does not mean \emph{zero}: zero filling suppresses the
very demands whose load the controller must anticipate, while explicit
matrix completion is optimized for reconstruction error and lets that error
propagate un-damped into routing~\cite{zhang2009}.
Second, observations have both spatial and temporal structure. Demands
originating from nearby satellite footprints are correlated, and recent
snapshots contain information absent from the current mask; yet mixing
observed and imputed entries in a graph model lets the imputed ones
contaminate the shared representation~\cite{t2gnn}. Third, a practical
model must remain usable as topology snapshots and active demand sets
change, without retraining for every orbital state. Learning directly from
sparsely measured matrices has been demonstrated on terrestrial
networks~\cite{test}, but sparse observation at constellation scale
additionally requires topology-aware representation and size-invariant
deployment.

We present \sysname{} (\underline{Spa}rse-aware \underline{T}raffic
\underline{E}ngineering), a learned allocator designed around these
constraints. It represents each unobserved demand with a learnable mask
embedding rather than a fabricated volume. A gated flow-centric graph
neural network (GNN)~\cite{teal,kipf2017} lets unobserved demands receive
link-state information without injecting their \emph{placeholders}, the
surrogate values that stand in for unmeasured demands at the model input,
into link embeddings. A temporal Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017} encodes
recent sparse observations, and per-demand cross-attention fuses temporal
and spatial evidence before predicting path split ratios. Shared weights
and demand pruning keep the model independent of the instantaneous demand
set, enabling reuse across constellation snapshots and scales.

After allocation, \sysname{} can optionally refine residual overloads using
link loads estimated from the same sparse observation. The important input
to this lightweight step is a neighbor estimate: an unmeasured demand is
inferred from observed demands sharing its source satellite. This estimate
uses geographic locality to avoid granting the controller an unavailable
ground-truth traffic matrix. It is an implementation aid to the sparse-aware
allocator rather than the organizing principle of the system.

We evaluate \sysname{} on a 1{,}584-satellite Starlink Shell-1
constellation over observation ratios from $2\%$ to $50\%$, measuring the
realized maximum link utilization (MLU) against ground-truth demands. Because
a learned allocator is a random variable in its initialization, we treat the
training initialization as an experimental factor rather than a nuisance
parameter: every configuration is trained from several independent
initializations and compared under matched conditions. \sysname{} holds the
realized MLU in the $1.64$--$1.89$ range, within $1.19$--$1.37\times$ of a
path-restricted LP optimum, while deciding in under $0.4$~s per snapshot and
transferring to a one-third-scale constellation without architectural change.
Against impute-then-optimize pipelines and zero-filled temporal baselines the
outcomes are mixed and no single pairwise comparison reaches significance; we
therefore report the full distributions over training initializations and use
them to delineate the regime in which sparse-aware allocation helps and the
regime in which simple fill-based treatments remain competitive.

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sparse-observation TE as a partial-observability decision
    problem.} We recast traffic engineering under partially observed demands
    as direct decision making rather than a two-stage estimate-then-optimize
    pipeline. The key enabler is that a controller emits per-path \emph{split
    ratios}, which are multiplicative in each demand's volume, so a demand can
    be routed without knowing its magnitude. This turns the apparently
    ill-posed sparse problem into a well-posed control problem whose
    objective, the realized MLU under ground-truth demands, stays well defined
    even when those demands are never measured.
    \item \textbf{A sparse-aware allocator.} We design an allocator that
    treats missing traffic as a first-class signal rather than reconstructing
    it: learnable mask embeddings replace unobserved demands, an
    asymmetrically gated flow-centric GNN lets those demands read link state
    without writing fabricated volume into it, and a temporal Transformer
    fused with the spatial embedding by per-demand cross-attention supplies
    the information the current mask omits, all without an explicit
    traffic-matrix completion stage.
    \item \textbf{Guarantees that hold regardless of the training outcome.}
    We prove two structural properties of the architecture that do not depend
    on how well any particular model is trained: \emph{placeholder isolation},
    whereby a poorly learned prior for missing demands can never corrupt the
    allocation of measured traffic, and \emph{size invariance with permutation
    equivariance}, which lets one trained model be reused across constellation
    snapshots and scales without retraining.
    \item \textbf{An evaluation that maps the operating regime.} We evaluate
    at Starlink scale across observation ratios from $2\%$ to $50\%$, treating
    the training initialization as an experimental factor rather than noise:
    every configuration is trained from independent initializations and
    compared under matched conditions with paired significance tests. Rather
    than reporting a single favorable number, we chart where sparse-aware
    allocation helps and where simple fill-based treatments remain
    competitive, and release the full distributions behind every comparison.
\end{itemize}
