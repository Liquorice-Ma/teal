\IEEEPARstart{L}{ow-Earth-orbit} (LEO) satellite constellations are rapidly
scaling toward tens of thousands of satellites, carrying user traffic with
pronounced spatial and temporal fluctuations over inter-satellite links
(ISLs) of limited capacity. Without global coordination, shortest-path or
purely distributed routing concentrates traffic on a few congested ISLs,
leaving network capacity underutilized. Traffic engineering (TE) ---
centrally optimizing how each demand is split across candidate paths --- has
therefore become a key control primitive for satellite networks, just as it
has been for cloud wide-area networks (WANs) over the past
decade~\cite{teal,smore,dote}.
