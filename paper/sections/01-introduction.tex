\IEEEPARstart{L}{ow-Earth-orbit} (LEO) satellite constellations are rapidly
scaling toward tens of thousands of satellites~\cite{handley2018,
bhattacherjee2019,giuliari2020}, carrying user traffic with
pronounced spatial and temporal fluctuations over inter-satellite links
(ISLs) of limited capacity~\cite{kassing2020,michel2022}. Traffic
engineering (TE) --- centrally optimizing how each demand is split across
candidate paths --- has become a key control primitive for these
networks~\cite{jain2013,hong2013,hong2018,sate}. A recent line of
learning-based TE systems makes centralized control practical at scale:
neural allocators replace online solvers and cut allocation time from
minutes to milliseconds~\cite{teal,dote,harp,sate}. Because a neural
allocator can emit allocations that overload individual links, these
systems almost universally append a \emph{repair layer} --- a few steps
of a convex procedure such as ADMM~\cite{boyd2011}, or a lightweight
rebalancing heuristic, that restores the raw allocation to
feasibility. Under sparse traffic
observation, the learned component carries a second responsibility: since
only a subset of demands is metered, it is also expected to
\emph{impute} the missing demand information, whether through learned
mask embeddings~\cite{teal}, synthetic augmentation~\cite{test}, or
explicit completion stages.

This second responsibility is unavoidable in LEO constellations.
Network-wide measurement requires every satellite to meter, aggregate,
and downlink fine-grained flow statistics through bandwidth-constrained
telemetry channels, competing with user traffic and consuming scarce
onboard resources; measurement gaps due to intermittent ground contacts
are the norm rather than the exception. In practice, the controller
observes a \emph{sparse} traffic matrix --- a fraction $\rho$ of demands
measured at each epoch --- and must still allocate \emph{all} traffic.
Filling unobserved entries with zeros leaves no headroom on the links
that unmeasured traffic actually traverses, inflating the realized
maximum link utilization (MLU); two-stage complete-then-optimize
pipelines propagate reconstruction errors into allocation
decisions~\cite{test}. Learned imputation is therefore the standard
answer: the allocator is trained end-to-end to act well despite missing
inputs.

The two responsibilities --- imputation and allocation --- are trained
into one network, while feasibility is enforced by the repair layer.
This decomposition carries a tacit but load-bearing assumption:
\emph{the components compose}. The learned allocator is assumed to
contribute whatever improvement it can learn from data, and the repair
layer is assumed to add, on top of it, the correction of residual
violations --- two positive, additive terms. Every system that couples a
neural allocator with a repair layer~\cite{teal,sate,dote} implicitly
relies on this assumption when it reports end-to-end performance: the
measured gain is attributed to the pipeline as a whole, and no attempt
is made to separate what the learned part contributes from what the
repair part contributes.

This paper shows that the assumption fails, and characterizes precisely
how. Our vehicle is \sysname{} (\underline{S}parse-\underline{i}nput
\underline{T}raffic \underline{E}ngineering), a representative
sparse-input TE system that combines learnable mask embeddings,
mask-aware gated message passing, temporal-spatial cross-attention,
and a constraint-repair layer (\cref{sec:design}). We conduct a controlled
mechanism study of \sysname{} on a
1{,}584-satellite Starlink constellation (Shell-1 configuration) driven
by real traffic dynamics, holding the architecture fixed and sweeping
observability $\rho$, the repair budget, training status, and random
seeds. \textbf{(RQ1) Does learning contribute when repair is present?}
No. The repair layer \emph{absorbs} the learning signal, and it does so
as a \emph{switch} rather than a dial: a single repair sweep collapses the
training gain from $+28.1\%$ to $-3.3\%$, and two or five sweeps change
nothing further. Once that repair is in place a trained network is
indistinguishable from an untrained one (9 of 25 paired snapshots won;
sign test $p=0.115$ in the untrained direction, and a $95\%$ interval
on the paired gain of $[-5.5\%, +0.6\%]$ that rules out any improvement
beyond $0.6\%$). \textbf{(RQ2) When does learning contribute without
repair?} At \emph{every} $\rho$ tested: training wins 13 of 15
paired cells across $\rho \in [0.02, 0.5]$
(two-sided sign test $p=0.018$), improving MLU by
$+28.1\%$ at $\rho = 0.3$ and $+9.8\%$ at
$\rho = 0.1$; even at $\rho = 0.02$ two of three
seeds improve. No collapse boundary exists --- the mask embeddings
regularize allocations even at 2\% observability. \textbf{(RQ3) Does the
mechanism depend on constellation scale?} A one-third-scale instance of
the same constellation architecture reproduces the absorption
phenomenon (\cref{sec:eval}). \textbf{(RQ4) What does repair need to
know?} More than the policy does. To rank congested paths, the repair
step needs demand volumes, and the implementation inherited from the
fully-observed setting takes them from the \emph{ground-truth} matrix
--- an information advantage over a policy that sees only a fraction
$\rho$ of it, and one that no sparsely-measuring controller could
reproduce. Restricting repair to the observation it can actually see
costs it a substantial part of its advantage, which reopens the space
the learned component was supposed to occupy. Together, the results
imply that the learned component and the repair component are
\emph{functional substitutes}, not complements --- and that the
substitution is underwritten by an information asymmetry that the
literature's evaluation protocol leaves invisible.

The findings redraw the boundary of where learned TE adds value, and
they carry concrete guidance. \emph{For evaluation}, a learned TE
system paired with strong repair must be benchmarked in the repair-free
regime or against repair-only baselines; end-to-end numbers conflate
the two components and can certify a trained model whose weights
contribute nothing. \emph{For design}, if repair is kept for safety,
the learning budget should target what repair provably does not
provide --- we show that the one robust residual advantage of the
learned system is \emph{stability}: it wins 17/25 paired
snapshots against zero-filling, with markedly lower variance and a
worst-case MLU up to 38\% better at extreme sparsity --- rather
than median performance. \emph{For deployment}, the absence of a
collapse boundary means training remains worth its cost even at
2\% observability; and the operative choice is not whether to pay for
repair but which matrix to hand it --- the variant that erases the
learning signal is precisely the variant a sparsely-measuring
deployment cannot run.

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We identify the \emph{repair--learning composition assumption}
    tacitly made by learned TE systems that couple a neural allocator
    with a constraint-repair layer, and design a controlled protocol ---
    varying training status, repair budget, observability, and seeds in
    isolation --- to test it (\cref{sec:formulation,sec:eval}).
    \item We show the assumption fails via a mechanism we call
    \emph{repair absorption}: repair acts as a switch --- one step
    collapses the entire learned differential, and further steps add
    nothing --- while in absolute terms that same repair is what
    delivers most of the performance; without repair, learning is
    effective across the entire observability range tested, with no
    collapse boundary even at $\rho=0.02$ (\cref{sec:eval}).
    \item We characterize what learning \emph{does} still provide --- a
    stability advantage in variance and worst-case MLU --- and
    translate the findings into guidance for evaluating, designing,
    and deploying learned TE under sparse observations, validated
    across constellation scales (\cref{sec:eval,sec:conclusion}).
    \item We show that the inherited repair layer owes much of its
    strength to reading the ground-truth traffic matrix --- a
    dependence that is unremarkable under full observability but
    unavailable, and to our knowledge never isolated, under sparse
    measurement. Making the matrix repair reads an explicit factor
    separates the repair \emph{algorithm} from the repair
    \emph{information} (\cref{sec:deployable}).
\end{itemize}
