\IEEEPARstart{L}{ow-Earth-orbit} (LEO) satellite constellations are rapidly
scaling toward tens of thousands of satellites~\cite{handley2018,
bhattacherjee2019,giuliari2020}, carrying user traffic with
pronounced spatial and temporal fluctuations over inter-satellite links
(ISLs) of limited capacity~\cite{kassing2020,michel2022}. Traffic
engineering (TE), which centrally optimizes how each demand is split
across candidate paths, has become a key control primitive for these
networks~\cite{jain2013,hong2013,hong2018,sate}. Classical
solver-based TE recomputes allocations in minutes, far too slow for a
topology and demand pattern that shift on the order of seconds, so a
recent line of learning-based TE systems replaces the online solver
with a neural allocator and cuts allocation time to
milliseconds~\cite{teal,dote,harp,sate}, making centralized control of
constellation-scale networks practical.

These systems, however, assume a \emph{complete} traffic matrix as
input, and this assumption is uniquely fragile in satellite networks.
Obtaining a network-wide traffic matrix requires every satellite to
meter, aggregate, and downlink fine-grained flow statistics through
telemetry channels that compete with user traffic for scarce ISL and
ground-station bandwidth, while consuming limited onboard computation
and power; intermittent ground contacts further leave measurement gaps
as the norm rather than the exception. A constellation controller
therefore observes a \emph{sparse} traffic matrix, a fraction $\rho$
of demands metered at each decision epoch, yet must still allocate
\emph{all} traffic, including the unmeasured part. Conversely, a TE
system that acts well on sparse observations lets the operator
deliberately meter only a small demand subset and reclaim the
telemetry budget, which is exactly why sparse-observation TE is a
natural operating mode for LEO constellations rather than a degraded
one.

Sparse observation breaks existing learning-based TE in two places.
First, the allocator: feeding a model trained for complete matrices
with zero-filled input conflates ``unmeasured'' with ``no traffic''
and leaves no headroom on the links that unmeasured demands actually
traverse, inflating the realized maximum link utilization (MLU), while
two-stage complete-then-optimize pipelines propagate reconstruction
errors into allocation decisions~\cite{test}. Second, and largely
overlooked, the feasibility-repair step: because a neural allocator
can emit allocations that overload individual links, learned TE
systems append a few sweeps of a convex or heuristic repair
procedure~\cite{teal,boyd2011}, and the repair implementation
inherited from the full-observation setting ranks congested links
using the \emph{ground-truth} traffic matrix, information that a
sparsely measuring controller does not possess. Under sparse
observation, the design question is therefore twofold: how should the
learned allocator represent what it cannot see, and what information
should the repair step be given so that it remains effective when fed
only the observation.

This paper presents \sysname{} (\underline{Spa}rse-aware
\underline{T}raffic \underline{E}ngineering), a learning-based TE
system designed end to end around the observation actually available.
On the allocator side, \sysname{} represents each unobserved demand
with a learnable mask embedding instead of a fabricated volume,
propagates link state through a mask-aware gated graph neural network
so that unobserved path nodes receive congestion information without
injecting placeholder traffic, and encodes the history of sparse
observations with a temporal Transformer fused into the graph
representation by cross-attention. On the repair side, \sysname{}
restricts the rebalancing sweeps to a traffic estimate computed purely
from the observation, filling each unobserved demand from observed
demands that share its source. The two components add up: the learned
allocator supplies a sparse-aware allocation prior that already avoids
most overloads, and the observation-fed repair corrects the residual
violations; the choice of the estimate that feeds repair, a
neighbor-based fill rather than zeros, is what keeps the repair step
effective at low observability.

We evaluate \sysname{} on a 1{,}584-satellite Starlink Shell-1
constellation with population-driven traffic dynamics, sweeping the
observation ratio $\rho$ from $0.02$ to $0.5$ and comparing against
zero-filling and mean-interpolation adaptations of full-observation
systems and a TEST-style sparse baseline~\cite{test}, all methods
paired with the identical deployable repair layer. \sysname{} achieves
the lowest realized MLU at every observation ratio tested

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate the sparse-observation TE problem for LEO
    constellations, in which the controller meters only a fraction
    $\rho$ of demands per epoch yet is evaluated by the realized MLU
    that all traffic, observed or not, induces on the network.
    \item We design \sysname{}, a sparse-aware learned allocator that
    combines learnable mask embeddings, mask-aware gated message
    passing, and temporal cross-attention, so that missing
    measurements are represented explicitly rather than filled with
    fabricated volumes.
    \item We identify that the feasibility-repair step inherited from
    full-observation TE reads the ground-truth traffic matrix, and we
    design a deployable alternative whose rebalancing is driven by a
    neighbor-based estimate computed from the observation alone,
    showing how the information source handed to repair determines
    its effectiveness under sparsity.
    \item We evaluate \sysname{} at Starlink scale across observation
    ratios from $2\%$ to $50\%$, demonstrating consistently lower
    realized MLU than sparse adaptations of existing systems,
    millisecond-level allocation latency, and retraining-free
    transfer across constellation scales.
\end{itemize}
