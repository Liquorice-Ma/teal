\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a controlled mechanism study of learned traffic
engineering under sparse observations, using \sysname{} as the vehicle.
The central finding is \emph{repair absorption}: when a convex repair
layer is present, it saturates within a single ADMM iteration and
absorbs the entire learned signal, rendering training indistinguishable
from random initialization (9/25 paired wins on a 1{,}584-satellite
Starlink constellation; 3/6 on a one-third-scale instance). The
absorption is global --- it persists across observability levels,
random seeds, and constellation scales.

Without repair, learning is effective across the entire observability
range tested (13/15 paired wins, $\rho \in [0.02, 0.5]$), but the
gain profile is topology-dependent: the learning peak sits at
$\rho=0.3$ on the main constellation and shifts to $\rho=0.05$ on the
smaller, denser instance. The one robust residual advantage of the
trained system is stability --- lower seed spread and better worst-case
MLU (up to +38\% at extreme sparsity) --- not median performance.

These results carry direct guidance. For evaluation, learned TE systems
paired with repair must be benchmarked repair-free or against repair-only
controls; end-to-end numbers under repair conflate the two components.
For design, if repair is retained for safety, the learning budget
should target tail robustness rather than median improvement. For
deployment, training remains worthwhile even at 2\% observability; the
real question is whether to pay for repair at all.

Three directions remain open. First, the repair-absorption interaction
likely generalizes to other projection mechanisms (dual-based
projection, feasibility layers in differentiable optimization); a
unified theory of when repair dominates learning would inform
architecture design. Second, the stability advantage suggests a
dedicated training objective --- explicitly optimizing worst-case MLU
rather than mean --- may unlock what the current loss function leaves
on the table. Third, measurement scheduling (deciding \emph{which}
demands to observe next) becomes especially valuable in the no-repair
regime where each observation directly informs the learned allocation.
