\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% 性能立意结论：问题回顾 → SpaTE 方案与设计要点 → 结果总结 → 展望。

This paper addressed traffic engineering for LEO satellite
constellations under sparse traffic observation, the operating mode
that onboard resource limits and telemetry costs impose in practice: a
controller that meters only a small fraction of demands must still
allocate all traffic within milliseconds. We presented \sysname{}, a
learning-based TE system designed end to end around the observation
actually available. On the allocator side, \sysname{} represents
unobserved demands with a learnable mask embedding, gates their message
passing so that placeholders never contaminate link states, and fuses
the history of sparse observations with the spatial graph
representation through per-demand cross-attention. On the repair side,
\sysname{} identifies that the feasibility-repair step inherited from
full-observation systems implicitly reads the ground-truth traffic
matrix, and replaces that dependence with a neighbor-based estimate
computed purely from the observation, showing that the information
source handed to repair, rather than the repair budget, is what
determines its effectiveness under sparsity.

On a 1{,}584-satellite Starlink constellation with population-driven
traffic, \sysname{} achieves the lowest realized MLU among all sparse
adaptations of existing systems at every observation ratio from $2\%$
to $50\%$ under the identical deployable repair layer
(\todo{headline numbers from overall\_main.csv}). The two components
are complementary: learning improves the allocation prior at every
observability level, repair corrects residual overloads, and the
neighbor-fed estimate recovers most of the benefit of the undeployable
oracle repair. \sysname{} additionally offers markedly lower seed
variance and better worst-case MLU than zero-filling, transfers to a
one-third-scale constellation without retraining, and infers in under
$0.4$~s per snapshot with 4{,}468 parameters, comfortably within a
constellation controller's compute envelope.

Three directions remain open. First, replacing the heuristic
rebalancing with a differentiable feasibility layer trained jointly
with the allocator may let the two components share, rather than
split, the observation. Second, the stability advantage suggests a
dedicated training objective that explicitly optimizes worst-case MLU
rather than the mean. Third, measurement scheduling, deciding
\emph{which} demands to observe next, is a natural extension: once
both the allocator and the repair estimate are driven by the
observation, each additional measurement improves them jointly, and
the observation budget itself becomes a control variable.
