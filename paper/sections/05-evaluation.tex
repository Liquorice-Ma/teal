\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Constellation.} Our main testbed is a Starlink Shell-1 Walker
constellation~\cite{giuliari2020} interconnected in a $+$Grid pattern
(\cref{tab:constellation}); orbital dynamics are derived from the
Satellite Tool Kit (STK). With 1{,}584 satellites, an average path length
of 23.51 hops, and a diameter of 47, capacity conflicts between demands
are far more frequent than in the terrestrial WANs used in prior TE
studies~\cite{teal}. For the cross-scale check
(\cref{sec:cross-scale}) we use a one-third-scale instance of the same
architecture (24 planes $\times$ 22 satellites, 528 nodes).

\textbf{Traffic.} Traffic matrices are generated by random city
sampling~\cite{giuliari2021}: cities are drawn with probability
proportional to their population, each sampled city pair is attached to
its nearest satellites, and per-pair flows are aggregated into
satellite-level demands (\cref{tab:tm-stats}). We use 101 consecutive
snapshots (one every 5 minutes), with snapshots 0--79 for training,
80--89 for validation, and 90--100 for testing, a strict temporal
split with no leakage. ISL capacity is calibrated so that the
fully-observed optimum leaves moderate congestion, keeping TE decisions
non-trivial. Demand pruning (\cref{sec:scaling}) restricts the modeled
demand set to the ${\sim}6.3$K pairs that ever carry traffic, out of
2.5M node pairs.

\textbf{Metric and protocol.} Our metric is the \emph{realized MLU}
evaluated against ground-truth demands (lower is better); flow
conservation in \cref{eq:conserve} is enforced by construction, so MLU
cannot be gamed by under-serving traffic. We sweep the observation
ratio $\rho \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5\}$ and run three random
seeds per configuration. Unless stated otherwise, every method is
paired with the \emph{identical deployable post-processing layer}: two
rebalancing sweeps whose utilization estimate reads only the sparse
observation with neighbor fill (\textsc{obs-nbr}, \cref{sec:training}),
so that no method receives unavailable demand information and the
allocator remains the only factor that varies. The \textsc{oracle} variant,
which reads the ground-truth matrix, appears only in the neighbor-estimation
sensitivity analysis. Because seed-to-seed
variation is comparable to between-configuration differences, headline
comparisons are \emph{paired} on $(\rho, \text{seed})$ and assessed
with two-sided sign tests; we report win counts and medians rather
than pooled means.

\textbf{Baselines.} All learned baselines share the training pipeline,
path sets, and optional neighbor-assisted refinement of \sysname{} and differ
only in how they handle sparsity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{zero-fill}: the sparse adaptation of full-observation
    systems such as Teal~\cite{teal} and SaTE~\cite{sate}, which
    cannot run on an incomplete matrix; unobserved entries are set to
    zero and the model is trained end to end on the filled input.
    SaTE's heterogeneous-graph allocator targets the same
    MLU objective with the same pruning, so this variant is its
    closest sparse-input proxy.
    \item \emph{mean-interp}: a two-stage complete-then-optimize
    baseline that fills unobserved entries with the mean of observed
    demands before allocation.
    \item \emph{nbr-fill}: a stronger two-stage baseline that fills
    each unobserved demand from observed demands sharing its source
    satellite, the same estimate used by the optional refinement.
    \item \emph{TEST-inspired}: a controlled baseline motivated by
    TEST~\cite{test}, with zero-filled input, a Transformer over the
    observation history ($L=3$), and an ungated GCN. We use the same paths,
    objective, and training pipeline as the other methods rather than
    claiming reproduction of TEST's original terrestrial setup.
    \item \emph{untrained}: the \sysname{} architecture with weights
    frozen at random initialization, isolating the contribution of
    training.
    \item \emph{full observation}: \sysname{} given the complete
    matrix ($\rho=1$), an upper reference on input information.
    \item \emph{LP oracle}: the per-snapshot optimum of
    \crefrange{eq:mlu}{eq:mlu-nonneg} solved on ground-truth demands,
    a lower bound no online method can beat.
\end{itemize}

The results reveal two operating regimes. At moderate observability,
\sysname{} achieves the best median MLU, leading all evaluated allocators at
$\rho=0.3$ and $0.5$. Across the full range, it also lowers median MLU
relative to zero filling by $1.3\%$ to $7.9\%$ and beats zero filling in
11 of 15 seed-matched cells. At lower observability, the TEST-inspired
baseline has the best median, leading \sysname{} by $0.5\%$, $5.7\%$, and
$5.2\%$ at $\rho=0.02$, $0.05$, and $0.1$, respectively. Thus temporal
history is especially valuable when the current observation is extremely
small, whereas \sysname{}'s mask-aware spatial representation becomes more
effective once enough demands are visible. These complementary regimes are
more informative than a claim of uniform dominance and identify extreme
sparsity as the main remaining limitation.

\subsection{Isolating the Sparse-Aware Allocator}
\label{sec:allocator-isolation}

The main table evaluates the complete deployed pipeline. To isolate the
learned allocator, we compare \sysname{} with the same architecture frozen
at random initialization and also evaluate both without post-processing.
With the common neighbor-assisted refinement enabled, training wins 9 of 15
seed-matched cells. Its median benefit is largest at $\rho=0.5$ ($13.0\%$)
and $\rho=0.02$ ($9.8\%$), while the middle ratios are close or mixed. This
shows that deployment-time refinement can compress differences between
allocation priors and that the end-to-end result should not be attributed
to either component alone.

Without post-processing, training improves realized MLU in 13 of 15 paired
cells (two-sided sign test $p=0.018$). The median gain reaches $28.1\%$ at
$\rho=0.3$ and remains $16.5\%$ at $\rho=0.02$
(\cref{fig:rq2}). The sparse-aware representation therefore learns a useful
allocation prior even when the current observation contains only $2\%$ of
demands; the deployed result additionally depends on how residual
congestion is estimated.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the Neighbor Estimate}
\label{sec:neighbor-estimate}

The optional refinement pass needs an estimated traffic matrix only to rank
candidate paths by congestion. \Cref{tab:ladder} holds the allocator fixed
at random initialization and varies this estimate at $\rho=0.3$. Zero
filling reduces median MLU from $2.833$ to $2.295$ but remains blind to
unmeasured load. The source-neighbor estimate reaches $1.740$, recovering
most of the improvement obtained by the unavailable ground truth
($1.599$). Thus the practical lesson is about estimation: source-local
traffic correlation provides a substantially better congestion signal than
zeros, without requiring complete matrix reconstruction.

\subsection{Cross-Scale Generality}
\label{sec:cross-scale}

To test whether the allocator depends on constellation scale, we reuse its
size-invariant architecture on a one-third-scale Shell-1 instance with 528
satellites and 2{,}112 ISLs. \Cref{tab:cross-scale} isolates the learned
allocation prior without post-processing at two observation ratios. At
$\rho=0.05$, training lowers median MLU from $4.02$ to $3.04$ and wins all
three paired seeds. The result at $\rho=0.3$ is mixed, indicating that the
most informative observation regime shifts with traffic density. The model
requires no architectural change as the graph and demand-set sizes change,
which supports reuse across constellation scales while avoiding a claim of
uniform gains.

\subsection{Inference Latency}
\label{sec:runtime}

On the 1{,}584-satellite constellation, one complete allocation pass
(demand-set assembly, graph rebuild, gated GNN, temporal encoder, fusion,
policy head, and optional neighbor-assisted refinement) completes in under
$0.4$~s per snapshot on a single GPU, against a 5-minute control period.
Combined with the $O(D)$ complexity of \cref{sec:complexity} and the
4{,}468-parameter model size, \sysname{} fits the compute envelope of a
constellation controller.

\subsection{Takeaways}
\label{sec:takeaways}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \sysname{} improves median MLU over zero filling throughout the
    tested range and is best among all evaluated allocators at
    $\rho=0.3$ and $0.5$; the TEST-inspired baseline remains stronger at
    lower observation ratios.
    \item The mask-aware allocator learns a useful allocation prior in
    isolation, winning 13 of 15 paired cells without post-processing, while
    the deployed end-to-end result depends on the observation regime.
    \item Source-neighbor estimation gives optional post-processing a much
    better congestion signal than zeros and approaches the unavailable
    ground-truth reference without making it the paper's central mechanism.
\end{itemize}
