\begin{abstract}
Low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite constellations require traffic
engineering (TE) that allocates traffic within milliseconds over a
network whose topology and demand shift continuously. Existing
learning-based TE systems achieve this speed, but they assume a
complete traffic matrix as input, whereas onboard resource limits and
telemetry downlink costs mean a constellation controller can meter only
a small fraction of demands at each decision epoch. Under such sparse
observation, completing the matrix with simple zero or mean filling
degrades allocation quality, and the feasibility-repair step that these
systems inherit implicitly reads traffic information unavailable to a
deployable controller. This paper presents \sysname, a sparse-aware
traffic engineering system that jointly designs the learned allocator
and the repair layer around the observation actually available.
\sysname represents unobserved demands with a learnable mask embedding
rather than fabricated values, propagates link state through a
mask-aware gated graph neural network, encodes the history of sparse
observations with a temporal Transformer, and drives repair with a
neighbor-based demand estimate computed purely from the observation.
On a 1{,}584-satellite Starlink constellation with population-driven
traffic dynamics, \sysname achieves lower maximum link utilization than
zero-filling, mean-interpolation, and TEST-style baselines across
observation ratios from $2\%$ to $50\%$
(\todo{median improvement from overall\_main.csv}), while retaining
millisecond-level inference and transferring across constellation
scales without retraining.
\end{abstract}
