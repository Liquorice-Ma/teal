\begin{abstract}
Learning-based traffic engineering (TE) systems increasingly pair a
neural allocator with a repair layer that restores allocations to
feasibility, and under sparse traffic observation --- the
norm in low-Earth-orbit satellite constellations, where only a subset
of demands is metered --- the learned component is additionally
expected to impute the missing demand information. The two components
are tacitly assumed to compose: learning refines the allocation and
repair fixes whatever remains. We show that this assumption fails.
In a controlled study of a sparse-input TE system on a
1{,}584-satellite Starlink constellation with real traffic dynamics,
we find that the repair layer \emph{absorbs} the learning signal:
repair behaves as a switch rather than a dial --- a single repair sweep
collapses the training gain from $+28\%$ to zero and further sweeps
change nothing --- so with repair present a trained network is
indistinguishable from an untrained one (9/25 paired wins, and a
$95\%$ interval on the paired gain that rules out any improvement
beyond $0.6\%$).
Learning pays off only in a repair-free regime, but does so across
the entire observability range tested (13/15 paired wins,
$\rho \in [0.02, 0.5]$), peaking at $+28.1\%$
at $\rho = 0.3$; no collapse boundary exists even at
$\rho = 0.02$, where two of three seeds still improve. The learned system's remaining edge over
zero-filling is stability --- lower variance and better worst-case
maximum link utilization --- rather than median performance. A
one-third-scale constellation instance reproduces the same mechanism.
Finally, we show that this repair layer --- inherited from the
fully-observed setting it was designed for --- silently consults the
\emph{ground-truth} traffic matrix to rank congested paths, information a
sparsely-measuring controller does not have. Restricting repair to the
observation it can actually see removes much of its advantage, which
moves the operative question from whether learning helps to which
component is allowed to see the traffic.
These findings redraw the boundary of where learned TE adds value and
yield concrete guidance for combining learning with feasibility
repair.
\end{abstract}
