\begin{abstract}
Low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite constellations require traffic
engineering (TE) that reacts quickly to changing topology and demand.
Existing learned TE systems achieve low online latency but assume a complete
traffic matrix, whereas telemetry and onboard-resource constraints allow a
constellation controller to meter only a fraction of demands at each epoch.
This paper presents \sysname, a sparse-aware TE system that allocates all
demands directly from incomplete observations. \sysname combines learnable
mask embeddings, asymmetric graph gating, temporal encoding, and
cross-attention so that missing measurements are represented explicitly and
historical and spatial evidence are fused per demand; a source-neighbor
estimate additionally supports optional deployment-time refinement without
access to the ground-truth matrix. On a 1{,}584-satellite Starlink
constellation, \sysname lowers median realized maximum link utilization
relative to zero filling at every tested observation ratio from $2\%$ to
$50\%$, with reductions of $1.3\%$ to $7.9\%$, and achieves the best median
among all evaluated allocators at observation ratios $0.3$ and $0.5$.
Results at lower ratios also expose the remaining challenge of extreme
sparsity, where a TEST-inspired temporal baseline can be stronger.
\sysname runs in under $0.4$~s per snapshot and transfers across
constellation scales without architectural changes.
\end{abstract}
